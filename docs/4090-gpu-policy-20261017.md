# RTX 4090 capability policy, memory budget and tuning

## Scope and safety of the policy

The application now requests the WebGPU capacities that it actually uses, then
validates the **effective `device.limits`** before building its optical pipelines.
This includes the storage/uniform binding counts and sizes, texture dimensions,
workgroup dimensions, invocation count, shared memory and dispatch dimensions.
Statistics can fall back from 256 to 128 or 64 threads on a smaller device. Large
sampling dispatches and statistics partials can use two dispatch dimensions, with
the padded last row masked by the shader. Optional work counting has its own
binding/shared-memory capability check.

Limits describe legal allocations and dispatches, not a speed score or available
VRAM. See the [WebGPU limits specification](https://gpuweb.github.io/gpuweb/#limits)
and [device descriptor](https://gpuweb.github.io/types/interfaces/GPUDeviceDescriptor.html).

The configurable logical budget defaults to **4 GiB including a 768 MiB reserve**.
Managed buffers and RGBA16F optical output are counted; the reserve estimates
Three.js render targets, textures and driver resources that are not individually
tracked here. This is an application policy, **not a query of free physical VRAM**,
and cannot guarantee that a driver allocation will succeed. Runtime diagnostics
and the UI explicitly label it an estimate. Supply `memoryBudget: {budgetBytes,
reserveBytes}` to `createHybridRuntime` to change it.

Allocation checks include the old and new images/BVHs coexisting, statistics
partials, timestamp buffers and readback staging. A rejected image or BVH change
keeps the previous valid resources. Temporary reservations are released on failure
and disposal; a rejected statistics request does not poison subsequent requests.
The hardware's per-buffer and binding-size limits remain separate checks.

## Interpretation of hardware counters

NVML GPU utilization is busy time. It cannot be multiplied by 83 TFLOPS to obtain
achieved FP32 throughput. PIX SM throughput, FMA pipe activity and active warps
describe the specific replayed dispatches. The FMA pipe also handles integer and
other operations, so these are not exact retired FP32 FLOP counters. See
[NVIDIA's pipeline definitions](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-compute/ProfilingGuide/index.html).

Stall shares below use `smsp__warps_issue_stalled_REASON.sum / smsp__warps_active.sum`;
they are approximate hardware-counter measurements, not a source-line diagnosis.
[NVIDIA's stall definitions](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-graphics/UserGuide/shader-profiler.html#stall-reasons)
distinguish memory-dependency long scoreboard, coupled-math dependency wait and
shader barriers. D3D12 ResourceBarrier EOP attribution is the difference between
consecutive completion timestamps, **not isolated barrier latency**. TOP-to-EOP
timings overlap other events. See [PIX timing definitions](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/pix/gpu-captures/).

Statistics alone are **35.6× faster (97.2% less GPU time)** in this replay comparison;
this confirms the previous task's multi-workgroup fix, not a new speedup from the
current capability/budget policy. Main reduction is now close to the device's
reported bandwidth throughput, followed by a tiny final scan. Its ~21% shader
`barrier` share is workgroup reduction synchronization, distinct from D3D12
ResourceBarrier. It is no longer the dominant event in this capture.

There is **no demonstrated register-count reduction**. The hardware average
register metric varies by a few registers across replays; it is not a static SASS
register declaration. Optical long-scoreboard is lower in the new captured chunks,
while math-dependency and instruction-supply waits remain large. Because adaptive
chunk sizes, pixel/accumulation positions and counts differ between captures, the
stall change cannot be attributed solely to one source edit. Optical durations
are 38–40 ms and 129.7 ms for the three new dispatches; the long third event repeats
in both replays and is retained. These durations are not a direct old/new shader
speedup experiment. Use the controlled full-application benchmark for speed claims.

Barrier EOP attribution varies considerably: old 0.17 to 3.61 ms and new 0.29 to
1.41 ms across replays, even though the recorded command lists are unchanged.
One large attributed value belongs to a constant-buffer transition. These cannot
be called isolated barrier latencies or a measured barrier regression. The stable
TOP→EOP sums, zero optical shader-barrier stalls and the much larger optical
dispatch costs make barrier changes a low priority. Fullscreen draws total about
0.68 ms EOP attribution in each capture. The extra new barriers/dispatches include
the added reduction pass and an additional sampling chunk, so raw counts are not
a normalized regression metric.

**Working diagnosis:** the remaining GPU work is dominated by the fused optical
and scene kernel, with coupled arithmetic dependencies, instruction-supply waits
and low effective warp/SM activity. High register use is still a constraint to
investigate, but these counters do not prove register spilling or a specific
instruction-cache miss cause. Optical DRAM throughput rounds to 0%, with 99% L2
hits, so its data do not support saturated VRAM bandwidth as the main bottleneck.
VRAM capacity and D3D12 barriers are not the limiting factors demonstrated here.
Further optimization should target dependency chains/code size/effective parallel
work, and be accepted only after full-scene timing and output checks.

The exact achieved TFLOPS and full-render FP32 peak-utilization percentage remain
**unmeasured**: no reliable retired FP32 FLOP counter is exposed in this export.
The 83–90% normal GPU busy time and the **capture-specific** ~4% SM / ~2% FMA Heavy
activity answer different questions. They show substantial room to improve this
workload, but do not establish an actual `83 × percentage` TFLOPS value.

The exporter confirms the 1,536,000,000-byte accumulation buffer and 6000×4000
RGBA16F output (192,000,000 bytes). Exported committed-resource constructors
reconstruct replay resources; they do not prove Chrome/Dawn's original heap
allocation, fragmentation or residency. No claim of a physical VRAM leak is made.
